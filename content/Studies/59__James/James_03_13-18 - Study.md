### 4d. Genre-specific questions
#### Module: Epistle
**Questions**
- E1. Who wrote it, to whom, and from where? →
- E2. What occasion or problem is being addressed (reconstruct from text)? →
- E3. What is the author's relationship to the readers? Tone? →
- E4. Where does this passage sit in the argument (opening, thanksgiving, doctrine, practical, closing)? →
- E5. What is the main verb or main assertion? Which clauses are subordinate? →
- E6. What does each "therefore", "for", "but", "so that", "since" connect? →
- E7. Is this indicative (what God has done) or imperative (what we must do)? →
- E8. What is the link between the indicative and the imperative? →
- E9. Which statements are theological, which practical? →
- E10. What slogans, creeds, hymns, or quotations are being used? →
- E11. Which OT passages are quoted, and how are they used? →
- E12. Are the readers' own words or objections being quoted back (diatribe)? →
- E13. Is a command specific to that situation or transcultural? Apply the criteria table. →
- E14. Who is addressed with each "you" (singular vs. plural, individuals vs. church)? →
- E15. What does it say about the church (corporate vs. individual)? →
- E16. Which doctrines are assumed vs. argued? →
- E17. What is the role of the Spirit, Christ, and the Father in the argument? →
- E18. What does it say about suffering, hope, and the end? →
- E19. What is the purpose of the whole letter (state in one sentence)? →
- E20. What would the readers have done on receiving this? →
- E21. What parallels exist in other epistles? →
