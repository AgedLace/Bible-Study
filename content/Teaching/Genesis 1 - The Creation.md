## Big Idea

God created everything by His spoken word, declared it good, and made humanity in His image to rule over creation.

## Introduction

- Hook: Show a picture of a vast galaxy or deep-sea creature — ask "Who made this?"
- Context: Genesis is the book of beginnings; chapter 1 answers "Where did everything come from?"
- Thesis: God is the sovereign Creator who speaks, orders, and blesses His creation

## Body

### Point 1: God creates by His word
- Explanation: Ten times in Genesis 1, "And God said" precedes a creative act. God does not struggle or labor — He speaks, and it exists.
- Illustration: A king's decree vs. a craftsman's labor — God rules by decree, not effort
- Application: Trust God's word — if He could create worlds by speaking, He can speak into your situation

### Point 2: God declares creation good
- Explanation: Seven times God evaluates His work as "good" (Hebrew: towb). Creation is not flawed or evil; it reflects God's character.
- Illustration: An artist stepping back to inspect a finished painting — each brushstroke is intentional
- Application: Value creation — the physical world matters to God; stewardship matters

### Point 3: God makes humanity in His image
- Explanation: Genesis 1:26-28 — humans created male and female, in God's image, with dominion over creation. This is the climax of creation.
- Illustration: A king's image-statue placed in a province — humans represent God's rule on earth
- Application: Every human bears God's image — treat all people with dignity and respect

## Conclusion

- Summary: God created all things by His word, declared them good, and made humans in His image to represent Him
- Call to action: Worship the Creator; steward creation well; honor the image of God in every person
- Prayer: "Lord, help us see Your glory in creation and reflect Your image to the world"

## Discussion Questions

1. What does it mean that God created "by His word" rather than by effort?
2. How does seeing creation as "good" change how we treat the environment?
3. What does it practically look like to bear God's image in daily life?

## Pitfalls to Avoid

- Don't get bogged down in the age-of-the-earth debate — the text's main point is God as Creator, not the mechanics
- Don't elevate humanity above creation's purpose — we rule as God's representatives, not as owners
